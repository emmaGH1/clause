# Build Exact Change

Give a judge a naira budget and ask how much rice it buys. Let them guess before Kane opens the shop. The winning frame is a real pack, its shelf price, the quantity the shop actually accepts, and the cash left over. A judge can change the budget and challenge the answer. That gives **Verified** and **Closed loop**, the two tiebreakers, something concrete to score. [Official rules and judging](https://www.kaneclihack.sh/)

## Eight one-action pitches

1. **Exact Change.** When a Lagos shopper taps their cash budget, they get the most rice that budget buys among three packs at one online shop, and TestMu gets Kane trying the quantities in a guest basket and an agent revising the shopping list from the observed subtotal.
2. **Hold the Reveal.** When a webtoon artist taps the panel that should end a teaser, they get a shareable cut that preserves the last line and conceals the next panel, and TestMu gets Kane reading the published cut while an editor agent moves the crop and asks for a second reading.
3. **Field Sheet.** When a geology student picks a field area, they get the map and matching cross section in a phone-readable field packet, and TestMu gets Kane following a survey publication into its sheets while an agent uses the printed legends to select and verify the right pair.
4. **Pidgin Pickup.** When a market seller pastes a public pickup pin, they get a Pidgin direction card ending at a named landmark, and TestMu gets Kane opening the map as a visitor and an agent replacing any direction whose landmark Kane cannot find on the shared view.
5. **One Vote Left.** When a DAO member taps a proposal, they get the voting deadline in Lagos time and the eligibility message the voting page shows for their public address, and TestMu gets Kane inspecting a supported read-only governance UI and an agent following its delegation link when the page reports zero voting power.
6. **Credit Roll.** When a zine editor drops in a Wikimedia Commons image link, they get a paste-ready credit from that file’s author and license record, and TestMu gets Kane tracing the attribution chain and an agent revising the printed credit when the source points to a different creator than the uploader.
7. **Last Bus Home.** When a meetup host picks a closing time, they get a departure card for one supported bus route, and TestMu gets Kane querying a public journey planner and an agent moving the suggested exit time earlier until Kane finds a bookable or scheduled trip.
8. **Find My Bubble.** When a lettering editor taps a translated line, they get the exact comic panel where a reader first encounters it, and TestMu gets Kane stepping through the published reader and an agent narrowing the search when the same line appears twice.

## 1. Exact Change

- **First 30 seconds.** Tap a budget on a phone. See three real pack options from the last timestamped run and make a guess. Start a fresh basket and watch the first Kane step arrive. On completion, tap the winning list to put the observed store subtotal beside the naira left in hand. Label the amount as groceries before delivery.
- **Why Kane matters.** A shelf price supports an estimate. The promise is a particular quantity accepted at a particular subtotal in a guest basket. The worker gets that observation only by sending Kane through the live storefront. Supermart.ng has a public food catalogue and rice category. Guest-basket behavior is the first unknown. [Supermart.ng](https://www.supermart.ng/)
- **Closed loop.** Kane captures pack weight, price and displayed availability. A coding agent calls a small integer optimizer for the heaviest combination under budget, then sends Kane the quantities. Kane stores the actual line counts and subtotal. If the shop caps a quantity or changes a price, the agent updates that constraint, recomputes, and asks Kane to try the revised list in an empty basket. Publish the accepted list with its timestamp. A claim of “most” requires comparable observations for all three packs and a reconciled optimum. Otherwise label the result “confirmed basket.”
- **Hour 1.** From the worker, have Kane add two units of one real pack, read back the line count and subtotal, then start a clean basket and try a different quantity. Compare both captures with the step screenshots. An inaccessible guest cart or ambiguous naira amounts on the chosen storefront are reasons to switch merchants immediately.
- **2½ days.** Day one buys the full journey for one merchant, three comparable plain-rice packs and one budget. Day two adds the optimizer, quantity revision, three budget presets and the side-by-side shelf/cart proof. The last half-day goes to fresh judge runs, a recorded fallback and submission. Stop at subtotal. The shopper completes any purchase separately.
- **TestMu winner copy.** “A shopper gave Exact Change a naira budget and got a rice list they could take straight to the shop. Kane tried the quantities in a real guest basket and gave the agent the evidence to settle on a list the budget actually covered.”

## 2. Hold the Reveal

- **First 30 seconds.** Open an original six-panel strip, tap the cliffhanger panel and open the generated teaser. Swipe to the end. A judge can read the final line, then open the full strip to see exactly what the teaser kept secret. Put the last verified browser frame beside the artist’s chosen panel.
- **Why Kane matters.** The artist is buying a reader’s-eye proof of the public link. A crop coordinate cannot show that the final balloon survived image scaling or that a sliver of the reveal escaped into the teaser. Kane supplies the actual rendered frame.
- **Closed loop.** The artist identifies the final balloon and a distinctive visual detail in the next panel. The agent proposes a crop. Kane opens the guest link, scrolls to its bottom and supplies a screenshot. The editor agent compares those two regions with the source art, moves the cut to the next candidate gutter if needed, then sends Kane to the new revision. If no horizontal cut satisfies both conditions, return the conflicting frame to the artist. Keep the terminal frame from each attempted cut.
- **Hour 1.** Use original or permissioned art with a reveal that has no text. Test two candidate cuts on a fixed-width web reader. Give the editor only the source references and Kane’s screenshots and see whether it correctly distinguishes an intact balloon, a clipped balloon and a visible piece of the reveal. If it needs hidden DOM labels to pass, pick Exact Change.
- **2½ days.** Day one ships one real strip, a panel picker and a public image-based teaser. Day two adds gutter candidates, two bounded revisions and a before/after viewer. Finish with a second artist-selected ending, phone testing and the film. Scope the proof to the tested reader width.
- **TestMu winner copy.** “A webtoon artist picked the last panel fans could see before a spoiler. Kane opened the public teaser like a reader, giving the editing agent a frame it could use to keep the punchline intact and the reveal out of sight.”

## 3. Field Sheet

- **First 30 seconds.** Tap **Kabul North** and ask for the map and cross section. Open the returned pair side by side and tap the section line on the map to enlarge the corresponding profile. Then try **Springfield**. In Kabul North the cross sections sit on the map sheet itself. In Springfield they live on Sheet 2. This is a real difference in how two survey teams published their work. [USGS Kabul North](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sim/3120/) · [USGS Springfield](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sim3462)
- **Why Kane matters.** The packet promises the actual matching line and profile, with both printed endpoint labels visible. A catalogue entry can identify the publication while leaving the student hunting across a poster-sized PDF. Kane opens the rendered material and supplies the visual evidence for the pairing.
- **Closed loop.** Kane reads the publication’s sheet descriptions. The agent chooses a sheet, downloads it and generates numbered image tiles with a PDF renderer. Kane opens the tile viewer and captures the visible section identifier and endpoints. The agent uses that observation to select the matching profile tile, requests a wider crop if a label is cut off, and asks Kane to confirm both ends on the finished packet. Preserve the survey’s uncertainty wording, particularly on Kabul North’s speculative sections.
- **Hour 1.** Rasterize the real Kabul North sheet at a resolution that preserves the primes in the endpoint labels. Have Kane distinguish the two section lines from visible pixels and reopen the selected profile. A single wrong pairing is enough to drop this idea unless a larger crop resolves it.
- **2½ days.** Support two named publications, one profile per packet, cached source PDFs and a tiny tile viewer. Day one gets a legible pair from one report. Day two handles the second layout, source links and packet generation. Reserve the last half-day for a blind pairing check, a fresh run from a phone and the demo. The same selection routine must work on both layouts.
- **TestMu winner copy.** “Field Sheet turned a geological survey poster into a map and cross section a student could read on a phone. Kane followed the printed section labels across the survey sheets and gave the agent the evidence to pair the right views.”

## Runtime and submission

Use a phone-friendly Next.js front end on Vercel and an authenticated Kane CLI worker on the VM that polls for jobs. The documented web primitive is `kane-cli run "<objective>" --agent`. Ask explicitly to **store** named observations, read the terminal `run_end` event and process exit, and pass the captured values or step screenshots to the coding agent for its next decision. Stream step descriptions to the judge. Show a timestamped prior run while fresh work starts. The interactive flow, extraction quality and latency are hour-one tests for these proposals. [CLI README and output examples](https://github.com/LambdaTest/kane-cli#ndjson-output---agent-mode) · [Kane changelog](https://github.com/LambdaTest/kane-cli/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)

For whichever idea ships, give judges a working path to a fresh run and a recorded fallback.
