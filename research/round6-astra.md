## Who would use it?

The plausible user is the relative who gets a family message asking, “They say I can take my money out for free. Where does it say that?” They have a reason to send back a screenshot the sender can read without visiting the site. That is a concrete, occasional use. The repository establishes that Kane can find an answer. It provides no evidence yet that families would use this service twice or trust its interpretation.

## Why use this over a browsing assistant?

ChatGPT, Claude or Codex with browser access could find the same FAQ and give an excellent answer. Someone who only wants the answer has little reason to switch. The reason to use this product is the second reader. They can inspect exactly what was on screen when the report was written and see which parts of the forward remain open. A screenshot establishes what the operator published. Whether customers actually receive withdrawals is a different question.

That difference fits in two minutes if the judge sees the pitch, the expanded FAQ and the revised report in the first 20 seconds. A chat box followed by a risk summary would look like another scam detector. A promise of hallucination-free prose would also overreach.
