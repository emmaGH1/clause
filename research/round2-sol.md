## All six against the kill questions

### Exact Change

1. **Eligibility.** Shared paperwork is passable. Judge access hinges on a permitted, repeatable guest basket in the actual deployment. The retailer audit below gives no such path yet.
2. **Repeat use.** A Lagos shopper reruns when cash, prices or pack availability change. If delivery fees consume the remainder, the tool has only verified a merchandise subtotal.
3. **Kane's job.** With Kane off, the estimator can do arithmetic. The store-accepted quantity and subtotal disappear.
4. **Verification.** For a fresh budget, compare requested quantities with actual line counts and subtotal. Show an accepted basket and a real cap or discrepancy. A single accepted cart establishes feasibility. A maximum-weight claim also needs comparable stock/cap and price evidence for all three packs and a valid optimization model.
5. **Closed loop.** A returned cap, changed subtotal or unavailable SKU must trigger a changed constraint, recomputation and a fresh cart assertion. If the store accepts every proposed quantity, the demo shows confirmation but gives little evidence of adaptive behavior. A blocked browser is a dependency failure.
6. **Delivery.** Hour one needs a permitted merchant and two clean-session cart checks from the actual worker, with the second quantity derived from the first finding. With permission and access unresolved, three live SKUs, fresh carts and an authentic correction are poor halfway bets. Lead with expected versus accepted quantity and money left.

### Hold the Reveal

1. **Eligibility.** The shared gate is plausible with actual creator-owned or licensed source art and a logged-out teaser. Grant scope has to cover a public demo and any full-strip comparison. A generated strip made solely to demonstrate a known crop failure earns zero on Real.
2. **Repeat use.** An artist or editor reruns on every teaser cut or revision. They save a review pass on what readers actually see on a phone.
3. **Kane's job.** Disable Kane and the renderer can produce an image. The browser-verified, publishable teaser disappears.
4. **Verification.** Use two endings from one genuine strip. A reviewer should recognize the complete balloon and the omitted visual reveal from the same guest-reader evidence. Include a textless spoiler and a sliver at the crop edge. Visible pixels, with a human ground truth, are the oracle.
5. **Closed loop.** A clipped text line makes the editor extend the cut. A visible spoiler makes it retract the cut. Reopen the new revision and assert both conditions. If no cut fits, stop with the conflicting frame. Render a cropped asset and check the guest view at an explicitly tested width.
6. **Delivery.** Spike an intact cut, a clipped balloon and a leaking cut on real art in hour one. One strip, one phone width and bounded gutter candidates can ship by halfway if the vision check works. Open the final public frame next to the artist's selected ending in the first 20 seconds.

### Field Sheet

1. **Eligibility.** Both USGS publications exist. The raw publication paths produced different results across fetch clients. Prove that the actual worker can see legible source pixels. Keep versioned local copies of the genuine sheets with provenance and check reuse rights.
2. **Repeat use.** A geology student or instructor reruns for a different traverse or publication and avoids hunting through giant sheets and matching tiny labels by hand.
3. **Kane's job.** With Kane off, the app can show source tiles, while the evidence-backed line/profile pairing and verified packet disappear.
4. **Verification.** Let the judge change the section. Require a visible line identifier and endpoints on the map and profile, checked against the original sheets. Show rejection of a genuinely different profile or an unreadable crop. Use publication and edition IDs as well as labels.
5. **Closed loop.** Kane returns a missing or conflicting label. The agent changes the tile or zoom, then Kane confirms the packet rendered for a phone. A prefilled answer key plus a screenshot would reduce Kane to an after-the-fact witness.
6. **Delivery.** Spike the smallest label at real browser scale and capture structured evidence. The Kabul North print sheet is 77.3 MB, with two sections on Sheet 1 that USGS describes as highly speculative. Springfield places its profiles on Sheet 2. Supporting both layouts adds a second ingestion and verification path before the first is proven. Lead with the two matching labels side by side. ### Android handoff verifier

1. **Eligibility.** The shared gate is plausible, but this pitch needs an account with a working mobile path, installable APKs and a legitimate ticket the judge can verify without exposing support data. None is evidenced here.
2. **Repeat use.** A release engineer runs it on each candidate build or high-risk support deep link to catch routing regressions before rollout.
3. **Kane's job.** Disabling Kane removes the observed native-screen match that makes the build recommendation credible.
4. **Verification.** Supply a fresh authorized ticket ID. One real build should land on the exact ticket, another on a different screen or ticket. A seeded two-APK demo with hardcoded outcomes earns zero on Real as pitched.
5. **Closed loop.** Consume per-build suite verdicts with the actual screen ID and route. Select the candidate with a confirmed match, then replay it with a different valid ID. An install or hub failure yields no product verdict.
6. **Delivery.** Hour one needs a real device session, install, deep-link launch, readable ID, and the relevant structured replay evidence from the intended execution path. Device listing and dry run are insufficient. With no APKs or entitlements demonstrated, the solo halfway target is speculative. The proof frame is requested ID beside actual ID on both builds.

1. **Eligibility.** Local Chrome and a shared folder avoid paid device prerequisites. Prove the clean clone/pull, review and run-evidence join before investing in a dashboard.
2. **Repeat use.** An incoming QA engineer uses it at policy changes and release handoffs to learn what coverage is stale and what needs checking.
3. **Kane's job.** The current criterion loses its linked coverage verdict and browser evidence when Kane is disabled.

A generic red/green dashboard would lose the 20-second read. Show the old policy, new policy and actual browser proof together. A merchant-approved demo should use fresh isolated guest sessions, a bounded run budget, and stop at the subtotal screen. A shopping agent fits the event's public-web agent lane. A meaningful verification claim is “the store accepted these lines at this subtotal and the subtotal fits this budget,” with an observed discrepancy that changes the list and a verified rerun. Fetching prices or clicking Add to Cart for a recommendation is a weak showing. The August winner demo.studio gives precedent for Kane performing a user-facing job, provided the shipped result is independently inspectable. ## Strictly better hybrid for this clock

**Field Sheet Handoff** takes Field Sheet's actual survey data and QA shift's reviewed, evidence-linked handoff. Begin with Springfield only. It has two genuine cross sections and two distinct sheets, so a second judge-selected section tests the same matching logic without a second ingestion pipeline. Both PDF URLs returned HTTP 200 with PDF content type from this VM. Pre-rasterize source regions into versioned tiles, preserve source edition, sheet references and links, and leave the pair mapping for Kane's visible observations. The agent can try the candidate profile, use the returned label or mismatch to select the other one, and verify both labels on the rendered phone packet. Issue the packet with the source edition and the evidence for the exact output. A wrong first candidate should be the real neighboring profile the algorithm could choose. Drop shared-context sync, arbitrary PDFs and publishing accounts. **Inference:** this is strictly better for the 2½-day submission objective than the two-publication Field Sheet or the demonstration-only QA handoff. It preserves real evidence, a meaningful negative and an agent correction while raising Buildable from 1 to 2. It gives up one publishing layout. The nearest past winner by interaction is demo.studio. The distinguishing proof here is a rejected cross-document pairing, a corrected evidence-backed packet, and a fresh user-selected profile. ## Ranked top three

1. **Field Sheet Handoff · 9/10.** When a geology student selects a Springfield section line, they get a phone-readable map/profile pair they can trace back to the survey, and TestMu gets a Kane finding that changes the agent's selection and verifies the delivered packet. **Biggest risk:** Kane confuses small printed labels or drops a prime mark. **Hour 1:** serve actual Springfield map and profile tiles on a minimal page. Have Kane return the visible endpoints and evidence for a mismatched pair, then let the agent pick the other profile and have Kane confirm it. Compare both runs with the sheets by eye.
2. **Hold the Reveal · 8/10.** When a webtoon editor selects a teaser ending, they get a guest link verified to show the full final line while hiding the next panel, and TestMu gets browser evidence that drives a crop correction and confirms the revision. **Biggest risk:** the visual check confidently misses a slight spoiler leak. **Hour 1:** on permissioned art and at a fixed phone width, blindly classify a clean cut, a clipped balloon and a visible spoiler sliver from Kane captures, then rerun one corrected cut.
3. **Print before closing · 8/10.** When a patron enters a preferred branch and arrival time, they get a source-checked place to print or an explicit closure result, and TestMu gets official-page evidence that redirects the agent and verifies the alternative. **Biggest risk:** the agent reads ordinary hours and misses the system-wide exception. **Hour 1:** run actual Kane Chrome on SFPL hours, closures and printing instructions.
